Co-writing with opinionated language models affects users’ views

Jakesch · Bhat · Buschek · Zalmanson · Naaman

Proceedings of CHI 2023, pages 1–15

Read the full paper → PDF

The subtlest form of secondhand AI isn’t in what Alice sends — it’s in who she becomes. Writing alongside an opinionated AI can shift her own view without her ever noticing.

Aim

Test whether writing with an AI assistant that leans toward a particular opinion changes the writer’s own stated view afterwards.

Hypothesis

Users co-writing with an opinionated language model will absorb its stance and be more likely to report holding that view — without being aware of the influence.

Results

Shift

Writers moved toward the opinion their AI assistant favoured.

Unnoticed

Most did not realise the assistant had influenced their view.

Key findings

  • 01 People who wrote with an opinionated AI assistant were more likely to later report holding its view.
  • 02 The shift happened during the interaction, without participants noticing it.
  • 03 It is carried into their later human conversations — an altered sender, not just an altered document.

Implications

AI can change the sender, not just the message.

Bob may talk to an Alice whose judgment was quietly shaped by an AI she used earlier. There is no artifact to label and no disclosure to give — the AI’s contribution is in how she now thinks. This is the hardest mode of secondhand AI to see, or to guard against.
