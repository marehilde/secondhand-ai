On the conversational persuasiveness of GPT-4

Salvi · Horta Ribeiro · Gallotti · West

Nature Human Behaviour, 9(8):1645–1653, 2025

Read the full paper → PDF

Fluency used to carry information — a persuasive argument suggested someone who understood it. GPT-4 breaks that link: it can argue as convincingly as a person, and more so when it knows who it is talking to.

Aim

Test whether GPT-4 can change people’s opinions in debate as effectively as a human — and whether knowing something about the listener makes it more persuasive.

Hypothesis

Given basic sociodemographic information about an opponent, GPT-4 will tailor its arguments and match or exceed human persuasiveness.

Key findings

  • 01 GPT-4 changed participants’ views roughly as much as human debaters did.
  • 02 Given basic information about the listener, GPT-4 became significantly more persuasive than humans.
  • 03 Persuasive prose no longer signals understanding — it can be produced regardless of the reasoning behind it.

Implications

Fluency has stopped being a reliable cue.

When Alice’s message is polished and persuasive, Bob can no longer read that as a sign of how well she understands it. The signal he used to trust — that convincing prose meant careful thought — has been prised apart from the thought itself.
